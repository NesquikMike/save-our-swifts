Why it matters

Swifts need Wenhaston as much as we need them

We formed in 2018 having become aware of the recent rapid decline in our swift numbers as reported by the Suffolk Wildlife Trust.

“The swift is one of those birds it is easy to take for granted. Arriving in England for the summer to breed, the high soaring scimitar shapes in the sky and the screaming of the bird around the rooftops can slip into the background of everyday life. Yet over the last 25 years there has been a decline of over 50% of the birds we see.”

Suffolk Wildlife Trust · suffolkwildlifetrust.org/swifts

The UK Breeding Bird Survey shows rapid decline in swift numbers between 1994 and 2018.

The latest BTO (British Trust for Ornithology) breeding bird data is out. It shows a 7% decline between 2024 and 2025 for the Common Swift. It’s a 44% decline since 2014 and a 70% decline since 1995.

Our aims

Protecting sites. Building new ones.

The aims of our group are to raise awareness, keep nest sites safe, and help the colony grow.

  • Raise awareness about the threat and decline of swift numbers
  • Protect existing nest sites
  • Encourage owners to preserve or replace nest sites when they are threatened
  • Encourage planners and builders to install brick or wall nest boxes on any new development
  • Encourage householders to install nest boxes with calling devices on their property
  • Celebrate the arrival of swifts in May
  • Enjoy the presence of swifts as they nest and feed around the village
  • Boost insect life by making public and private green spaces more wildlife and wildflower friendly
  • Measure local swift numbers by carrying out an annual count (baseline count in 2018)

Village gatherings

Group Activities

The group meets at least twice a year to make plans. We also organise two Swift Counts each year to monitor numbers of birds residing in and flying above the village. Everyone is welcome to join us on the annual Swift Pint Walk.

Twice in July

Annual swift counts

Since 2018 we observe and count swifts flying overhead from various points around the village at the same day and time, so we are as sure as we can be that we are not double-counting. We survey twice — early July and late July — using a method introduced by Alan Miller. Figures below are maximum numbers from the late July count; they are indicative rather than absolute totals.

Open to all

Swift Pint Walk

Every year we hold a Swift Pint walk, open for anyone to join. It takes members around the village viewing nest sites and swift screaming parties, ending at The Star Inn, which has swifts nesting in its roof.

Dates are set at our planning meetings — get in touch to be added to the list.

Advice & resources

First aid for grounded swifts.

Most important

Want to give swifts a home?

  • Boxes work best high under eaves, out of reach of cats.
  • Leave known nest holes undisturbed during works if you can.
  • Calling devices can help attract birds to new boxes.
  • Ask us before drilling or sealing roof spaces in summer.

Questions about boxes or nest sites? Email Kate for advice.

If you find a grounded swift

Helping a grounded swift does not mean giving it a home. You may be able to give first aid, or refer it to a local specialist who can provide more specialised care.

Place the bird in a box with several small air holes — a shoe box with a lid is ideal — lined with a couple of layers of kitchen roll. Keep the box in a quiet, warm place away from noise and interference.

Then follow the Swift Conservation first aid guidance, or contact the group for advice on local specialist care.

Not sure if it is a swift? Watch this short video by Simon Hooton from Suffolk Wildlife Trust on telling swifts, house martins, sand martins and swallows apart.
